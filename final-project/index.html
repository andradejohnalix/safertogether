Recognize it

What does cyberbullying look like?

Cyberbullying is repeated harmful behavior using digital technology. It can happen in private messages, class group chats, games, or social media. A single harmful post can also be worth reporting, especially if others keep sharing it. Read UNICEF’s guide.

Targeted messages

Someone repeatedly insults a classmate in a group chat or sends threatening messages during a game.

Humiliating posts

A private photo is shared to embarrass someone, and others repost it or pile on with cruel comments.

Impersonation

A fake account uses a student’s name or photo to spread rumors or send hurtful messages.

Before you share, pause.

If a post humiliates someone or exposes private information, leave it out of the group chat. A private check-in can help more than a public reply.
